Few wild horse herds in the country are as accessible — or as photographed — as the **Onaqui herd**. Roughly 200,000 acres of BLM-managed range stretch from the foothills between Rush Valley and Vernon all the way to the Simpson Mountains, and the horses that live there are famously easy to find compared to most wild herds out West.

## Where to Look

**Simpson Springs**, along the historic Pony Express Road, is the single easiest spot in Utah to see the Onaqui horses. Bands are regularly spotted along the road between Simpson Springs and the Old River Bed, with smaller groups often seen closer to Dugway and Rush Valley itself.

## Tips for Viewing

- **Stay at least 100 feet away** and never approach, pet, or feed the horses — these are wild animals on public land, not a petting zoo.
- **Early morning and evening** are the best times to spot them, when temperatures are cooler and the herd is more active.
- **Spring** brings foals and is a favorite season for photographers.
- The road out to Simpson Springs is unpaved gravel — a high-clearance vehicle is helpful, especially after rain or snow.
- Bring binoculars or a zoom lens; a respectful distance is part of the experience.

## How Far Is It From Clover Creek Guest House
