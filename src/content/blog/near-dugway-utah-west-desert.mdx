Say "Dugway" to most Utahns and they think of one thing: the Army's Dugway Proving Ground, a testing installation that's been here since World War II. It's an active military base and not open for public visits — but don't let that stop you from exploring the area around it, because the drive out here is one of the most underrated stretches of road in the state.

## What's Actually Out Here

From Rush Valley, State Route 199 climbs over **Johnson's Pass** through the Onaqui Mountains before dropping into the Dugway area — a scenic, lightly-trafficked drive with long views across the West Desert. This corridor is also the front door to some of Utah's best off-the-beaten-path attractions:

- The **Onaqui wild horse herd**, which ranges through the foothills between Rush Valley and Simpson Springs
- The historic **Pony Express Trail**, a graded backcountry byway that starts nearby and runs west across the desert
- Wide-open BLM land for hiking, photography, and stargazing, with almost no light pollution

## Tips for Visiting

- **Fuel up first.** Tooele is the last reliable gas stop before you head west — don't count on services past Rush Valley.
- **Cell service is spotty** once you're past Dugway, so download offline maps ahead of time.
- **Bring water and a full tank of common sense.** This is high desert — hot and exposed in summer, and services are genuinely far apart.
- Respect the fenced boundary of the Proving Ground itself; the surrounding public land is what you're here for.
