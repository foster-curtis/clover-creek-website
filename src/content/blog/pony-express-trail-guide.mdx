Rush Valley isn't just close to the Pony Express Trail — it was part of it. **Faust Station**, the first home station west of Salt Lake City for Pony Express riders, once stood right here in Rush Valley, run by stationkeeper Henry Faust. Today, the **Pony Express National Historic Trail Backcountry Byway** retraces roughly 133 miles of the original 1860–61 route across Utah's West Desert, from Fairfield to Ibapah.

## What to Expect

- A maintained BLM gravel road following the historic route, with **14 marked station sites** and interpretive signage along the way.
- **Simpson Springs**, one of the best-preserved stops, with a reconstructed station building and a year-round BLM campground.
- A landscape that's changed remarkably little since 1860 — open range, distant mountains, and a real sense of how isolated this route once was.
- Overlap with the **Onaqui wild horse herd**, often visible right along the byway (see our [wild horse guide](/blog/onaqui-wild-horses-rush-valley)).

## Tips for Driving It

- This is a **high-clearance, gravel-road trip** — a standard sedan can usually manage it in dry weather, but conditions change fast after rain or snow.
- **Fill up on gas and water before you go**; there are no services along most of the route.
- Cell coverage is unreliable to nonexistent — bring a paper map or downloaded offline maps.
- Combine it with a stop at **Camp Floyd State Park** in Fairfield, a restored 1858 army post and former Pony Express station (see our [BYU & Provo guide](/blog/byu-provo-day-trip) for a route that passes nearby).

## How Far Is It From Clover Creek Guest House

The trail effectively starts at Rush Valley's doorstep — Faust Station's original site is just down the road, and Simpson Springs is about 25 miles away, roughly a 45-minute drive on the byway.
