The **Bonneville Salt Flats** are one of the strangest, most photogenic landscapes in the United States — roughly 12 miles long and 5 miles wide of blinding-white salt crust, straddling I-80 near the Nevada border. It's famous as the home of land-speed racing, but you don't need a race car to appreciate it.

## What to Do There

- **Walk out onto the flats** at the public access area (I-80 Exit 4, then a few miles north) and experience one of the flattest, emptiest places on Earth.
- **Photograph sunset or sunrise** — the reflective salt surface makes for dramatic, otherworldly light, especially with a thin layer of water on the crust.
- **Watch for racing events** — Speed Week and other events bring land-speed record attempts to the **Bonneville Speedway** section, typically in warmer months.
- Pair the trip with nearby **Wendover** for food, fuel, and a bit of history (see our [Wendover guide](/blog/wendover-utah-guide)).

## Tips for Visiting

- **Check conditions before you go.** The flats flood seasonally and can be closed or unsafe to drive on — the surface looks solid but isn't always.
- **Bring water and sun protection.** There's no shade, and the white salt reflects sunlight intensely.
- **Don't drive off marked areas** during racing events, and always confirm the flats are open to public access first.
- Cell service is limited out here — download directions ahead of time.

## How Far Is It From Clover Creek Guest House

About 110 miles from Rush Valley — roughly a two-hour drive west on I-80, through some of Utah's emptiest and most dramatic scenery.
